Beat Lab

Build MidiNES drum kits by ear: pick a beat, swap sounds while it loops, and let it vary them from your Sound Sorter pairings. Everything plays by NES rules.

1Beat

Click a name to edit that drum's sounds, ♪ to mute or unmute the row. Click the grid to change the beat (off → hit → accent → ghost; toms 1 → 2 → 3); your changes stay with this beat until you reset it. The triangle has no volume control on the NES, so the bass is as loud here as it really is; pulses at 7 are a lead, 5 still on top, 4 a mix.

2Kit

Click a drum's name in the grid (or ↑ ↓) to open its buckets: Main for normal hits, Accent for accented hits, Ghost for ghost notes (toms: Hi, Mid, Lo). The NES can't play a sample softer or louder, so dynamics come from different sounds. Drag sounds in from the list below, between buckets, or out to remove them; click one to hear it. Vary 0 always plays a bucket's first sound; higher shuffles through the bucket. Speed wobble plays hits a speed or more higher or lower (a little, or a lot when cranked). Hats and cymbal can use NES noise too (shown as MidiNES notes: long noise C2–D#3, metallic E3–G4). Hear plays one hit, accent or ghost the way the beat would (keys Z X C); Solo (S) plays just this drum in the beat. Kick and snare can add a noise layer: a short burst with each sample for the top end the sample channel can't play (longer on accents, shorter on ghosts). Choose from lets you hear the beat with Fat Chip choosing on its own: by size (accents from the biggest sounds, ghosts from the smallest) or anything at all.

NES rules: one sample at a time (kick, then snare, tom, percussion, cymbal, open hat, closed hat); a hat or cymbal that loses the sample channel moves to noise, anything else is dropped. One noise sound at a time too, so a closed hat cuts an open hat off.

3Sweep pad

B95–B127 played as one sound. Drag left and right to move through the parts, up and down for the speed; hold still to repeat a part, let go to stop unless Loop is on. Clicking glides from your last click to the new one through every part on the way.
