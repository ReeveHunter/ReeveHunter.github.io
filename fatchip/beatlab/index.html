Beat Lab

Build your own Fat Chip beats from any MidiNES sample. Load sounds onto 16 pads, then click steps in or record them live as the beat loops, from the keyboard or a pad controller. Save beats in banks of five for Fat Chip 64.

Part
Fat Chip layers

These are Fat Chip 64's live settings, previewed here. A hat layer replaces the hats lane. Accents set the noise hats' level and the Game Boy's velocity; the MidiNES samples have no level of their own. VARY swaps drum hits for close relatives (vocals, FX and tonal clips never change): low keeps it tame, high gets wild.

2Steps

Click a step to put the selected pad there (click again to take it off; right-click clears). The grey bars show how long each sample rings on the MidiNES, cut off by the next sample. Striped hits lose the step to the kick / snare / perc lane. While recording, hold Backspace to erase the selected pad's lane as the beat passes (Shift for both lanes).

3Pads and sounds

Click a sound to hear it; double-click (or drag it onto a pad) to load it into the selected pad. Hats and cymbals go in the hats lane, everything else in the kick / snare / perc lane.

4Beat and library

 

Your banks

A beat saves its name, tempo, swing, time signature (if it has one), Game Boy kit, its pads and both parts. The export carries your bank names too, for the Fat Chip 64 and Game Boy updates. Everything also stays in this browser.

Pads in

Keys 1–4, Q–R, A–F, Z–V play the pads. A controller's pads start out on notes 36–51 (bottom left up, like an MPC); Learn MIDI pads sets them any way you like, for the SP-404 MKII or the MPK Mini. Web MIDI needs Chrome or Edge.

In Split, the MidiNES plays the kick / snare / perc lane and the Game Boy the hats lane. The Game Boy plays its own kit's sounds, matched to each pad by bucket.
