Beat Lab

Build MidiNES drum kits from the decoded samples and hear them play, before they go into Fat Chip.

1Families and groups

Your categories, each split into groups of samples that sound alike (from the decoded data). Name the groups if you like; click a sample to hear it at its own pitch. Length and pitch are measured within each family: 0 = the shortest / lowest of that family, 100 = the longest / highest.

2Kit

Target: the length and pitch you want, within the family. Spread: how far from the target a substitute may be (0 = only the closest). Change: how often it substitutes, from never to every hit (on noise: a step higher or lower and ±1 volume). Speeds: which NES sample speeds it may use (faster = higher and shorter). With Change on, accents push toward longer, fuller hits, soft notes toward shorter ones, and beat 1 a little longer. Toms can be a Hi / Mid / Lo set tuned by their measured pitch; toms that come close together walk down the set. The sample channel plays one sound at a time: kick beats snare beats tom beats cymbal; a cymbal that loses moves to the noise channel. A kick with a noise hat is the kick sample plus noise; with a sampled hat it's one sample that has both.

3Play

Click the grid to change the groove (off → normal → accent → soft). Humanize nudges every hit a few ms either way and the noise volume by 1. Dilla time pulls the kick early, lays the snare back and swings the off-beat hats loosely toward triplets. The balance uses the NES's own channel weights, so the noise and pulse levels are the ones Fat Chip would send (0–15): pulses at 7 are a lead, 5 still on top, 4 a mix. With a keytar connected, its rhythm plays the kit live (its Start / Stop and clock set the bar); live hits can't come early, so Dilla delays the rest a little instead.

4Sweep pad

B95–B127 played as one sound. Drag left and right to move through the parts (each one runs into the next), up and down for the speed. Hold still to repeat a part; let go to stop, unless Loop is on.

5Kit code

—

The whole kit and its seed in one short code with a check character, to write down, share, or (later) type into Fat Chip. Groups are numbered by family, so a code means the same kit as long as the categories stay the same.
